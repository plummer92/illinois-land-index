The exporter uses a field allowlist, not a list of fields to remove.

A row must have a recognized vacant or unimproved class and a verified no-structure flag. Improved parcels, unknown classes, unknown class codes, and uncertain structures stay private. If a county has fewer than five qualifying land rows, the public file contains its counts and calendar only.

Illinois: a lien certificate, not immediate land

Illinois annual tax sales sell tax certificates. Bidders compete by bidding the penalty rate down from the statutory 9% ceiling. For redemption periods beginning after July 10, 2026, the ordinary period is three years; certain vacant non-farm and other qualifying property can use a one-year period. The certificate holder must later petition for a tax deed within the statutory window and strictly complete notice and service. Most valuable parcels should be expected to redeem, so a certificate is not a shortcut to predictable ownership.

35 ILCS 200/21-215, 35 ILCS 200/21-350, and 35 ILCS 200/22-30.

Indiana: certificate, redemption, then a deed petition

The ordinary Indiana sale conveys a tax-sale certificate and lien, not immediate title. The standard redemption period is generally one year. County-acquired and commissioner-certificate routes can have a 120-day period. After the applicable period and required notices, a certificate holder may petition for deed. County-specific notices and dates control.

See the Indiana State Board of Accounts county-auditor resources and the current county sale notice.

Tennessee: a court sale with redemption

Tennessee delinquent-tax property is sold under a court decree and remains redeemable after confirmation. For sales confirmed on or after July 1, 2026, Public Chapter 971 generally sets one year when the delinquency is three years or less and 90 days when it is older. The confirmation order and current statute control the individual case.

See Tennessee Public Chapter 971 / SB 1983 and the Tennessee courts delinquent-tax manual.

Title, lien, and physical risks remain

A sale can still involve defective notice, federal or other surviving interests, easements and covenants, later taxes, occupants, municipal or demolition obligations, environmental conditions, no legal access, and quiet-title or title-insurance expense. A map circle does not grant permission to enter land. Verify the legal description, court file, title path, access, zoning, utilities, flood risk, and current sale status without trespassing.

Federal liens require special notice analysis. See IRS Internal Revenue Manual 5.17.2.
